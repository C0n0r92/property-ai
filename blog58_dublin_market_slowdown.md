# Dublin Market Slowdown: Rising Prices Amid Declining Activity

## Executive Summary

Dublin's property market shows a significant slowdown in transaction volumes with 67% fewer sales and rental listings compared to the previous month, yet average property prices increased 13.4% to €693,000. Analysis of 321 recent sales reveals declining over-asking success rates dropping from 80.5% to 70.7%, while rental yields fell 10.2% despite 5.3% rent increases. D2 leads price growth at 121%, followed by D4 at 31%, demonstrating selective market strength despite broad-based activity declines.

## The Market Activity Slowdown

Dublin property market activity has experienced a sharp decline, with transaction volumes dropping 67% from 970 sales in the previous month to 321 sales in the current period (November 22-December 22, 2025 vs October 21-November 21, 2025). This slowdown affects both sales and rental markets equally, with rental listings also down 67% from 655 to 215 properties.

| Market Segment | Previous Month | Current Month | Change |
|----------------|----------------|---------------|---------|
| Sales Transactions | 970 | 321 | -67% |
| Rental Listings | 655 | 215 | -67% |
| Average Price | €611,133 | €693,872 | +13.4% |
| Over-asking Success | 80.5% | 70.7% | -9.8% |

The broad-based decline affects all Dublin areas, with suburban districts experiencing the most significant drops. D13 shows the largest decline at 91%, while premium areas like D6 and D4 also see substantial reductions.

## Price Increases Despite Market Slowdown

Despite significantly lower transaction volumes, Dublin property prices continue to rise, suggesting reduced supply and sustained demand from committed buyers. The average property now commands €693,872, representing a 13.4% increase from the previous month's €611,133 average.

Analysis of areas with sufficient transaction volumes (3+ properties) identifies D2 as the strongest performer with 121% price growth, followed by D4 at 31% and D9 at 19%. These increases occur despite overall market slowdown, indicating selective demand pockets.

## Declining Over-Asking Success Rates

The market slowdown coincides with deteriorating seller conditions, with over-asking success rates dropping from 80.5% to 70.7%. This represents a 9.8 percentage point decline, indicating increased buyer caution and reduced competition for properties.

Areas with the largest activity declines also show the most significant drops in over-asking success, suggesting that reduced market competition directly impacts seller outcomes. Properties are now selling closer to asking price, with some areas experiencing negative premiums.

## Strategic Implications

### For Sellers
- **Premium Positioning**: Focus on D2 and D4 areas showing sustained price growth despite market slowdown
- **Realistic Pricing**: Over-asking success rates have declined significantly; consider pricing closer to market value
- **Timing**: Current market conditions favor patient sellers who can wait for committed buyers

### For Buyers
- **Selective Opportunities**: Areas with activity declines may present more negotiation room
- **Value Areas**: Focus on areas with moderate price increases (D8, D6) rather than overheated markets
- **Long-term Holding**: Consider properties in growth areas like D2 and D4 for appreciation potential

### For Investors
- **Yield Compression**: Rental yields have declined 10.2%; focus on areas with rent growth potential
- **Quality over Quantity**: Reduced inventory suggests premium properties command premium rents
- **Market Timing**: Consider waiting for stabilization or focus on areas showing rental demand strength

## Conclusion

Dublin's property market demonstrates a classic slowdown pattern: declining transaction volumes and deteriorating seller conditions alongside rising prices. The 67% drop in activity affects both sales and rental markets, yet prices increased 13.4% overall, suggesting reduced supply and selective demand pockets. Areas like D2 and D4 show remarkable resilience with 121% and 31% price growth respectively, indicating these remain strongholds for property investment.

According to the Residential Tenancies Board Q3 2024 report, Dublin rental growth has moderated to 3.2% annually, yet current data shows pockets of stronger growth in premium areas (RTB Residential Tenancies Quarterly Report, September 2024). [https://www.rtb.ie/research-reports]

This market environment rewards strategic positioning over aggressive pricing, with buyers gaining negotiation leverage while sellers in premium areas maintain pricing power. The disconnect between activity levels and price growth suggests a market transitioning toward sustainable long-term trends rather than speculative momentum.

## Methodology

Analysis covers Dublin property transactions from November 22-December 22, 2025 (321 sales) compared to October 21-November 21, 2025 (970 sales). Data includes all property types with valid sale dates and price information. Geographic coverage spans all Dublin postcodes with minimum 3 transactions for reliable price trend analysis. Rental analysis includes properties with yield estimates (215 current vs 655 previous period).
