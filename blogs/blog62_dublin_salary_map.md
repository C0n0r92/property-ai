# Dublin's Salary Map: Exactly What You Need to Earn to Buy in Every Postcode

## Executive Summary

Analysis of 15,710 Dublin property transactions (2024–2025) reveals stark income barriers across the city. A single buyer needs a minimum salary of €73,125 to enter the cheapest postcode with a 2-bed apartment (D22), rising to €219,375 to access a 3-bed house in D4 or D6. Only 5% of Dublin's market is accessible to a single buyer earning €60,000 — the approximate average Dublin salary. This data maps the precise income required to meet Central Bank lending rules at every Dublin postcode, by property type and bedroom count.

---

## How Irish Mortgage Lending Works: The Central Bank Rules

The Central Bank of Ireland imposes two binding constraints on mortgage borrowers:

1. **Loan-to-Value (LTV):** First-time buyers can borrow up to 90% of the purchase price (10% deposit required)
2. **Loan-to-Income (LTI):** Maximum mortgage = 4x gross annual income

These rules create a straightforward formula:

> **Salary required = (Property price × 0.9) ÷ 4**

For a couple, lenders typically assess combined income:

> **Combined income required = (Property price × 0.9) ÷ 4**  
> **Each partner's share = (Property price × 0.9) ÷ 8**

A €400,000 property therefore requires:
- Single buyer: **€90,000 salary** (€360,000 mortgage + €40,000 deposit)
- Couple: **€45,000 each** (combined €90,000)

## Strategic Implications

### For Buyers: Choosing Your Postcode by Budget

**Under €70k salary (single) or €35k each (couple):**  
Market access is extremely limited. Target 1-bed apartments in D22, D24, D11, or D17. These four postcodes represent almost the entire accessible market on a single average-to-above-average salary.

**€70k–€90k salary (single):**  
The 2-bed apartment market opens up across D22, D24, D11, D17, D12, D15, and D9. This is the most efficient income bracket — €80k unlocks 21.7% of the total Dublin market. Beyond 3-beds, expect to consider D10, D17, or D22.

**€90k–€120k salary (single):**  
Full 2-bed apartment access citywide including D3, D8, and D7. Three-bed houses accessible in D10, D17, D22, D11. At €100k, over 42% of the market is within range.

**Couple, €60k each:**  
Roughly 60% of the Dublin market is accessible. Three-bed houses are achievable in 10+ postcodes including D24, D15, D11, D22. This is where family formation becomes financially viable.

**Couple, €80k each:**  
80% market access. Four-beds open in D24, D15, D11. Three-beds in D7, D8, D3, D9, D5, D12, D13. Only D4, D6, D14, D16, D18, D6W remain largely out of reach.

### For Sellers: Pricing to Maximise Buyer Pool

Every €50,000 added to asking price increases the required single-buyer salary by approximately €11,250. In competitive postcodes like D10 (92.5% over asking) and D11 (89.7% over asking), sellers have pricing power — but in areas like D1 (67.5% over asking) and D18 (77.6% over asking), the buyer pool thins at elevated price points.

### For Investors: Apartment Yields in Accessible Postcodes

The cheapest postcodes for entry (D22, D17, D24) also offer strong rental demand due to their affordability. Apartments in D22 at a median €261,000 represent the lowest capital outlay in Dublin for a 2-bed rental unit. With Dublin rent averaging €2,200+/month for a 2-bed (RTB Q3 2025 data), the indicative gross yield at that entry price is approximately 10.1% — the highest accessible yield bracket in the dataset.

## Conclusion

Dublin's property market has a stark income geography. The city splits roughly into three zones:

- **Affordable entry (D10, D17, D22, D24, D11):** Single buyers on €70k–€90k can access 2-bed apartments; couples on €60k each can reach 3-bed houses
- **Mid-range (D15, D12, D9, D8, D7, D5, D13):** Requires €90k–€120k single or couples earning €55k+ each; 3-bed houses at or above €440k
- **Premium (D3, D4, D6, D6W, D14, D16, D18):** Family houses require €140k+ single or €70k+ each for a couple; the apartment premium is lower but still demands €80k+ salary

For first-time buyers, the practical entry point is a 2-bed apartment in D22 or D24 on a salary of €60k–€65k — but only with a 10% deposit already saved. Couples jointly earning €120,000 unlock the majority of the market, including 3-bed family homes across the affordable southern and northern suburbs.

According to the Central Statistics Office, median gross earnings in Dublin stood at approximately €47,000–€52,000 per worker in 2024, meaning most single earners are priced out of all but the cheapest corner of the Dublin market (CSO Earnings and Labour Costs, Q4 2024. [https://www.cso.ie/en/statistics/labourmarket/earningsandlabourcosts/]).

## Methodology

- **Data source:** 43,929 Dublin property transactions from dashboard/public/data.json
- **Analysis period:** 2024–2025 transactions only (21,120 records; 15,710 with valid Dublin postcode)
- **Price filters:** €50,000–€5,000,000 to exclude data anomalies
- **Salary calculations:** Central Bank of Ireland LTI rule (4x gross income) + 90% LTV for first-time buyers
- **Property type filters:** Minimum 20 transactions per postcode/type combination for statistical reliability
- **Median used instead of average:** Median prices are reported throughout to reduce distortion from high-value outliers
