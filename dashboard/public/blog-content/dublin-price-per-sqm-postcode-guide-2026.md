# Dublin Property: What You're Actually Paying Per Square Metre in 2026

When buyers compare properties in Dublin, they almost always focus on the asking price. But the price tag alone tells you very little about value. A €450,000 house in D22 might give you 110 square metres; a €450,000 apartment in D7 might give you 70. Same budget, 57% more space — depending on where you buy.

We analysed 4,100+ live Dublin property listings with size data to build a comprehensive price-per-square-metre map across 22 postcodes. The gap between Dublin's most expensive and most affordable areas is over 2:1 — and that difference shapes everything from what mortgage you need to how much you can extend without paying a premium.

---

## Executive Summary

- **D4 and D6** are Dublin's most expensive by volume, averaging **€8,000–€8,250/sqm** — more than double D10 and D11
- **D10, D11, D22** offer the best space efficiency, with median prices under €4,000/sqm and strong house supply under €400k
- The **price-per-sqm gap between inner and outer Dublin is narrowing** as urban premium compresses: D8 and D7 now average €6,000–€6,200/sqm despite more affordable entry points
- **Larger homes don't cost proportionally more** — 150sqm+ properties average €1.56m, but per-sqm they're often competitive in mid-tier areas
- **West and North Dublin** deliver the best structural value: 40%+ of listings under €400k with house stock, not just apartments

## Three Zones, Three Strategies

### Zone 1: The Premium Belt (€6,000–€8,250/sqm)
**D4, D6, D2, D6W, D14, D18, D8, D7, D1**

These areas command Dublin's highest per-square-metre rates. D4 and D6 are in a category of their own at €8,000+/sqm — Ballsbridge, Rathmines, Ranelagh. You're not paying for space here; you're paying for postcode prestige and proximity.

What's interesting is that D8 (South Circular, Portobello, Inchicore) and D7 (Phibsborough, Stoneybatter, Cabra) now sit at €6,000–€6,200/sqm — solidly in the premium tier, yet their **median prices are €395k–€450k** respectively. That combination — high cost-per-sqm but relatively affordable entry prices — means buyers are getting smaller units. The average D8 listing with sqm data is just 75sqm. 

If you need space, these postcodes will frustrate you. If you need location and can live compact, the entry price remains viable.

### Zone 2: The Mid-Tier (€4,800–€6,000/sqm)
**D16, D5, D9, D12, D13, D20**

D9 (Glasnevin, Drumcondra) at €5,255/sqm is the standout in this band — historically seen as premium North Dublin, it's now genuinely mid-tier by price-per-sqm. Average size here is 96sqm with a median price of €480k: reasonable value for an established northside address.

D12 (Crumlin, Walkinstown) at €5,194/sqm with a median of €450k is arguably underappreciated. Median property size is 94sqm — larger than D8 at a lower per-sqm rate, with strong house stock and 3-bed availability.

D16 (Rathfarnham, Knocklyon) at €5,720/sqm skews south-suburban. Larger homes, bigger plots, but you're paying for it. Median price of €595k reflects that.

### Zone 3: The Value Tier (Under €4,800/sqm)
**D22, D11, D10, D24, D15, D17**

This is where buyers who prioritise **space efficiency over postcode prestige** should focus. Three stand out:

**D22 (Clondalkin, Lucan West)** — €3,976/sqm median, 87sqm average, median price €325k. 83% of listings come in under €400k and the majority are houses — Terraced, Semi-D, End of Terrace. 67 3-bedroom properties under €400k. This is the most consistent value zone in the dataset.

**D11 (Finglas, Glasnevin North)** — €4,045/sqm median, median price €325k. 75% of listings under €400k, 63 three-bed houses under €400k in live inventory. The area has significant regeneration activity and planning applications in progress — buyers who see this as a long-term hold are finding it increasingly difficult to ignore.

**D15 (Castleknock, Blanchardstown, Clonsilla)** — €4,544/sqm, 102sqm average size. The largest volume zone in the dataset at 273 listings. Not the cheapest per-sqm but the **largest average property size** in the sub-€500k bracket. If you're buying a family home and need 100sqm+, D15 delivers it at scale.

## The Property Type Factor

Price-per-sqm varies significantly by property type across all postcodes:

- **Bungalows**: €7,283/sqm — the rarest type and most expensive per metre
- **Detached**: €6,295/sqm  
- **Apartments**: €5,664/sqm
- **Terraced**: €5,530/sqm
- **Semi-Detached**: €5,380/sqm
- **Duplex**: €4,512/sqm — structurally the best space value if you can tolerate the format

Duplexes are often overlooked. At €4,512/sqm average, a 100sqm duplex prices out significantly cheaper than an apartment of the same size — and they're typically in the same development. Worth checking explicitly when searching.

## Practical Implications

**For first-time buyers with a €400k budget**: D22, D11, and D24 give you the most square metres. A €370k 3-bed house at €3,900/sqm in D22 buys you roughly 95sqm. The same money in D7 buys you ~65sqm.

**For investors**: D11 and D24 offer the strongest combination of low entry price-per-sqm and improving rental demand — both areas show consistent rental listings in the €1,800–€2,200/month range.

**For upsizers**: D9 and D12 sit in the sweet spot — mid-tier per-sqm cost with decent average property sizes (94–96sqm), established infrastructure, and access to the city centre under 20 minutes.

**For premium buyers**: D4 and D6 aren't going anywhere near €6,000/sqm anytime soon. If you're buying there, you're buying the postcode — the per-sqm rate is effectively a fixture premium, not a space play.

## What This Means Going into Spring 2026

Price-per-sqm is becoming a more relevant filter as buyers face sustained affordability pressure. With the Central Bank's mortgage rules capping loans at 4x income, a household earning €90k combined is working with roughly €360k in mortgage capacity. At D8 rates (€6,000+/sqm), that buys 60 square metres. At D22 rates, it buys 90+.

Space efficiency isn't just a nice-to-have — in the current market, it's a strategic lever. Buyers who shop by price alone risk buying significantly less home for their money than comparable properties in adjacent zones.

The data suggests the gap between premium and value postcodes on a per-sqm basis is currently at a five-year high. That creates both opportunity and risk: opportunity for buyers who move into undervalued areas before they re-rate, and risk for buyers overpaying per-sqm in prestige postcodes without the lifestyle justification to match.
