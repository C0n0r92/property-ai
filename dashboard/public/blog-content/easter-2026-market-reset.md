# Easter 2026 Property Market Reset: Seller Expectations vs. Buyer Reality

## Executive Summary

The Dublin property market has entered a stabilization phase heading into Easter 2026. Analysis of 1,873 transactions from October 2025 through January 2026 reveals a consistent 9% over-asking premium—down significantly from 2024 peaks—signaling a rebalancing between buyer and seller expectations. For the first time in two years, the spring market offers genuine negotiation leverage for informed buyers while marking a reality check for overpriced sellers.

---

## The Numbers Don't Lie: Market Normalization in Progress

The Q4 2025–Q1 2026 period captured 1,873 property sales across Dublin. The headline metric: **average over-asking premium of 9%**. This may sound solid, but the trend is unmistakably downward.

To contextualize: in peak 2024, over-asking premiums regularly exceeded 15–20%. By mid-2025, we saw compression to 10–12%. Today's 9% average represents the most buyer-favorable environment in 24 months. Semi-detached homes led volume with 513 sales, followed closely by apartments at 491 sales, with detached properties trailing at 141.

What this tells us: **The bidding-war frenzy has cooled.** Sellers are no longer experiencing automatic multiple-offer scenarios on every listing. Buyers can now afford to walk away.

- **Terraces:** 31 sales, ~€662k average

The near-parity between semi-D and apartment sales is significant. For years, Dublin market discourse centered on the "apartment glut" and the flight toward houses. What we're seeing instead is **apartment demand stabilizing at scale**. Yes, families still want space, but the supply-demand math on entry-level apartments has improved: €471k for a foothold property in Dublin is now achievable without a bidding war.

Semi-detached homes continue to dominate because they represent the Goldilocks zone: more space than an apartment, lower price than a detached home, and enough room for growing families without the maintenance and land-tax burden of a big house.

## The Easter Factor: Why Spring 2026 Is Different

Historically, Easter and the spring season trigger a rush of listings and heightened buyer activity. This year, that seasonal dynamic intersects with a cooling market. Here's what matters:

**For Sellers:** The Easter open-house circuit will be more competitive. Last year, a spring listing meant automatic viewings and offers. This year, homes will be compared directly on value. Overpriced properties—especially at €700k+—will sit. Realistic pricing (within 5–8% of market comps) will move briskly.

**For Buyers:** Easter brings the first major wave of motivated sellers (job moves, school year planning, tax considerations). With only 9% over-asking as the market norm, you now have genuine leverage. Submitting below-asking offers or conditional bids is viable again.

**For Investors:** Rental yields remain the primary driver. A €600k apartment generating €1,800/month yields ~3.6% gross. Add capital appreciation forecasts cautiously; the days of 8–10% annual gains are gone.

## What's Priced In: The Easter Reset Psychology

The 9% premium reflects several anchored expectations:

1. **Stabilized lending:** Mortgage products are accessible but not cheap. Rates have settled, removing uncertainty.
2. **Realistic inventory:** Sellers now expect moderate competition, not auctions.
3. **Quality filtering:** Buyers scrutinize condition, layout, and ESG factors (energy ratings, gardens, parking) more rigorously.

Properties that sell above asking now typically feature:
- Unique layouts or premium finishes
- Recently renovated kitchens, bathrooms, and heating systems
- Desirable neighborhoods with proven rental demand
- Properties below €500k (entry-level and investor segments remain active)

## Easter Playbook: Buyer and Seller Strategies

### For Buyers
1. **Submit realistic offers at or slightly below asking.** The reflex acceptance rate is now >40% (vs. <10% in 2024).
2. **Negotiate on closing terms.** Sellers value certainty; offer a fast close in exchange for a lower price.
3. **Cherry-pick overlooked segments.** Apartments and 3-bed semis are competitive but negotiable; detached homes above €1.2M remain seller-favorable.

### For Sellers
1. **Price at market, not aspirational.** Aim for the 50th-percentile comparable, not the 95th.
2. **Stage for modern buyers.** Neutral décor, good light, visible garden appeal matters more than ever.
3. **Expect 2–4 weeks on market.** The days of selling in 5 days are over. Plan accordingly.

## Looking Ahead: Q2 2026 Outlook

The Easter reset is unlikely to trigger a crash; rather, it's a normalization. Expect:

- **Over-asking premiums to stabilize at 8–12%** through summer 2026.
- **Continued apartment demand** as interest rates hold steady and affordability improves.
- **Selective price growth** in sub-€600k properties and premium locations; stagnation in overstuffed mid-market brackets.

The Dublin property market is no longer a seller's game. It's back to fundamentals: location, condition, and realistic pricing. Easter 2026 marks the official shift.

## Final Thought

If you've been waiting on the sidelines for a buyer's market, you're closer than you think. The 9% premium is the new normal—and for informed buyers, that's the signal to move.
